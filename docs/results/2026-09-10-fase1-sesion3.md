# 1. One algorithm, two figures (#63, ADR-0007)

> **Pre-rotation.** This section was written on 2026-09-10 at `435c1a3`, on a build that had neither
> the legend's second row (#65), nor the fill's track (#67), nor round 10 — the rotation of the Level
> axis (#80), the fold (#82), the parking band (#83), the datum as a per-node mark and the turned ink
> (#93). The drawing it describes has since changed in the ways listed in **section 2**, each with the
> commit or ADR that moved it. The section is kept as written, numbering included — nothing was
> renumbered — and where it says *two things* travel between the figures, ADR-0007 §3 has said three
> since #65 (section 5.1). One count was already off when written: #63's opening sentence, quoted in the
> header above, says the ADR lost to the build *in two places*, and §1.6 lists four. It was written
> that way; it is not padding added afterwards.

`ALG 15` is drawn twice by this app, and the two drawings are both correct. ADR-0007 is the decision
to **keep both and write down what travels between them**, rather than to rewrite either. This
section is the part of it a reader needs in prose: what you carry from one figure to the other, and
what you must not go looking for in the wrong one.

The ADR itself is `docs/adr/0007-dos-dibujos-de-un-algoritmo.md`, accepted 2026-09-10, and it is the
authority — this is its point 3 and point 4 in English, plus what checking it against the shipped
build changed.

## 1.1 Which figure you are looking at, and why that inverted

The **wide** figure (`wide-layout.ts`) teaches topology **by position**: carriers are the nodes that
touch the output bus along the bottom, every arrow points down because a route always drops the
chain depth, each branch gets a contiguous band of columns to itself so no line ever crosses from
one branch to another, and an operator at Level 0 is parked out to the right on a dashed stub that
ends nowhere. None of that is captioned. It is seen.

The **narrow** figure (`layout.ts`) is the depth-sorted 3 × 3 grid: the eight operators sorted
deepest-first and filled three to a row. It is the verified drawing — it has a specification and
`layout.spec.ts` — and it does not claim to teach role by position.

What changed this session is **which one the app opens with**. Until 3a (#58), the wide figure was
the resting state: the app started with no Medida, and with no Medida the algorithm took the whole
width; the narrow grid arrived as a consequence of a capture. Since 3a the two Ranuras decide the
composición, and the factory pair is `SCOPE` and empty — so the glass column is open at the first
frame, and **the narrow grid is now the drawing the app opens with**. The figure that teaches the
most about topology is now the one you have to ask for. That inversion is the whole reason ADR-0007
exists, and it happened sideways, inside a ticket whose subject was the scope's geometry.

## 1.2 What you carry from one figure to the other

Two things, and they cover more than the first draft of the ADR credited them with.

**The node, entire.** There is one component and one template behind both layouts, so the node's
five facts are literally the same in each: the identity (`OP1`…`OP8`) with the role word beside it,
the Level as the height of the luminous fill measured against the patch's shared ceiling datum, the
ratio, the spectral form as a glyph, and the operator's Hz for the live note. With them travel the
one provenance stamp that qualifies everything polled underneath it, the dash that an invalidated
figure leaves inside its kept outline (#56), and the inert open corner. The only difference is how
those facts are arranged inside the box: in the wide figure, when the algorithm's depth leaves less
height than stacking them needs, the node lays them in a row instead (`squat`). Same facts, same
words, different fold.

**The lines.** Every route, every carrier's drop onto the output bus, the bus itself with its
`OUT L/R`, and the feedback arc with its `FB n` are drawn in **both** figures — including the
dashed, inert arc at `FB 0` that #57 added, and the dashed route out of an operator that has been
cut to zero. So **«who modulates whom» is not the wide figure's privilege**: it is in both. The
first draft of the ADR said otherwise, which described the narrow grid as an inventory with no lines
in it. That is not what it is, and the ADR has been corrected.

## 1.3 What you do not carry

The **position**, in four specific ways. Everything on this list exists only in the wide figure, and
a reader who looks for it in the grid will find something that looks like an answer and is not one.

- **Role by place.** In the narrow grid the bottom row is not the carriers' row. The grid fills three
  at a time in depth order, so a row can mix two depths and a carrier can land halfway up. Role is
  said there by the word (`CARR` / `MOD` / `ZERO`) and by the outline's shape, never by where the
  node sits.
- **The shape of a branch.** In the wide figure each connected structure owns a band of columns and
  no line crosses between bands, because there is no line between them to cross. In the grid the
  branches interleave, and nothing marks where one ends and the next begins — a route may even have
  to hop over a neighbour to reach its target.
- **Depth as height.** In the wide figure one row is one depth. In the grid, depth only orders the
  reading — deepest first, by operator number within a depth — and cannot be read back off the
  position.
- **What being at zero looks like.** In the wide figure an operator at Level 0 leaves the branches
  entirely, goes right onto a stub that ends nowhere, and **its routes are not drawn at all**: a line
  carrying nothing is not drawn across the picture. In the grid it keeps its cell and what is drawn
  is its route, **cut** — dashed. Both say «this one is not sounding». They do not say it with the
  same thing, and only one of them says it with position.

## 1.4 How you ask for the wide figure, and the two widths it comes in

By pressing `KEEP IT BIG` in the algorithm panel's own header, or by emptying both Ranuras. Since 3b
(#62) those are **not the same box**, and it is worth knowing which one is on screen:

- **Pinned.** The middle lane closes completely and the diagram takes 1 070 px at a 1 280 viewport.
  There is no rail: the pin is its own handle and it is in the header of the panel it is about.
- **Both ranuras emptied.** The lane collapses to the rail — 52 px, the bench drawer's closed
  vocabulary stood on its end — so the diagram is 1 016 px, and beside it are two handles each
  labelled with the panel one press would put back.

Both are `wide()` and both run the same `wideLayout`; the drawing stretches to whatever room it has
(`preserveAspectRatio="none"`), so the topology reads the same in either. The difference that
matters to a reader is that the second one leaves a way back on screen and the first one does not
need to.

## 1.5 What reconciling them properly would cost, and why it was not done

Taking the positional vocabulary down to the narrow grid is the real reconciliation, and it remains
the right long-term exit. It was not done here because it is a redesign and not a polish pass, and
because of what it would be traded against: the grid is the only one of the two figures that is
verified against the table. Doing it means solving, in a 700 px box, the two problems the wide figure
solves with room — a band per branch and a row per depth — at a scale that has neither the width nor
the height for either. That is its own session with its own measurements in front of it.

Writing the decision down costs a document. Getting the redesign wrong costs the only checked drawing
there is.

## 1.6 What checking the ADR against the build changed

The ADR was written during the grilling that produced #54 — that is, **before** 3a and 3b shipped —
and #63 asks explicitly that whoever closes it check that it still says what was actually built.
Four things did not survive the check, and the ADR now carries the corrected versions:

- **«Who modulates whom» was listed as not carried.** It is carried: the grid draws every route, the
  bus drops, the bus line and the feedback arc. Corrected in points 3 and 4, and in the paragraph
  that describes the narrow figure.
- **The list of the node's facts named the stamp and omitted the operator's Hz.** The five facts are
  identity-and-role, Level, ratio, spectral form and Hz; the stamp and the ceiling datum travel with
  them but are not among the five. Corrected in point 3.
- **«The wide figure is reached by `KEEP IT BIG` or by emptying both ranuras» was written before the
  rail existed.** It is still true, but the two routes now produce different widths — 1 070 px and
  1 016 px — and one of them leaves a rail on screen. Corrected in point 5.
- **The discarded alternative «factory pair with both ranuras empty» argued that nobody would see a
  signal panel «until they discover the handle».** With #62's rail there is nothing to discover: the
  handle is on screen and it is labelled with what it will restore. The alternative stays discarded,
  but for the honest reason — the app would open with no signal drawn and one press in front of the
  first one — and not for a cost the build no longer has.

One thing was checked and left alone: `composition.wide()` is still what chooses between the two
drawings (`operator-diagram.ts`), exactly as the ADR says. What changed underneath it is that it is
now derived from the column's three-valued `shape()` — `ranuras` / `rail` / `gone` — so code that
needs to tell the pin from the rail must read `shape()`. `wide()` deliberately gives them the same
answer, because for the drawing they are the same answer.

## 1.7 What this section does not claim

**Nothing here was measured on the keyboard, and none of it needed to be.** Every claim is about
which figure draws what, and each is readable in `layout.ts`, `wide-layout.ts` and
`operator-diagram.html`. The two widths — 1 070 px and 1 016 px — are the grid's own arithmetic at a
1 280 viewport (`app.ts`, and `column-geometry.ts` for the 52 px rail), not a measurement of a
screenshot.

What is still owed is the thing this section cannot settle: whether a pianist who learns the
topology in the wide figure actually recognises it in the grid. That is the question the
reconciliation would answer, and it needs someone in front of the real screen with a real
Performance — not a document.

## 2.1 Retired

- **§1.2, and again in §1.6's second bullet — «the Level as the height of the luminous fill».**
  Measured with Level vertical in both figures and the fill being the card (no track: #67 came
  after). Moved by #80 → ADR-0008 §2.1: the axis is declared per composición (`levelAxis`), the grid
  keeps its height, the wide figure measures along the width from a shared left origin. In one of the

- **§1.1 and §1.3's fourth bullet — «parked out to the right on a dashed stub that ends nowhere», and
  «its routes are not drawn at all».** Measured with the stub as a column at the right, and
  `drawn(from) && drawn(into)` as one predicate. Moved by #83 → ADR-0008 §2.4 and §2.5: the parked
  operator leaves the depth stack into a band above the deepest row, at the *origin* end of the Level
  axis, and keeps its stub; a route *out of* it is not drawn, a route *into* it is — inert, dashed,
  onto the bar that closes the stub. ADR-0007 §4's principle stands and its direction is retired by
  name (ADR-0008 §6).
- **§1.1 — «every arrow points down».** Moved by #83: the route into a parked operator is the one line
  in the wide drawing that runs upward, and `wide-layout.ts`'s header says so.
- **§1.3's third bullet — «in the wide figure one row is one depth».** *Depth as height* is not what
  moved: ADR-0008 §2 keeps the vertical as depth and the fold exists because positions never fold.
  What moved is the row: since #83 `place()` lays out the *occupied* depths only, so the stack closes
  up behind a parked operator, and the parking band is a row that is no depth.
- **§1.2 — the `squat` node as the only thing that happens to a cramped card, and «same facts, same
  words, different fold».** Measured with `WIDE_ROWS = 8` drawn in full at every depth. Moved by
  #82 → ADR-0008 §4: **folding**, one mechanism with two triggers — six rows or more at the body's
  floor, or a card narrower than the 131 px the grid draws the same five facts in — under which the
  deepest operators keep their position and give up ratio, glyph and Hz. So a folded node does *not*
  hold the same facts, and the word *fold* in that sentence, which meant the arrangement inside the
  box, now names a mechanism that did not exist. This is also the sentence that falsifies ADR-0007 §3
  in a live document; that is #96 (section 5.3).
- **§1.2 — «the one provenance stamp that qualifies everything polled underneath it».** True of the
  unfolded node. #82's folded band wears one stamp for the band, the weakest of its four figures by
  `FRESHNESS`'s rank.

## 2.2 Corrected elsewhere and still true

- **§1.4, §1.6 and §1.7 — 1 070 px pinned at 1 280.** Never false on screen. Between #65 and #76 the

- **§1.4, §1.6, §1.7 — 1 016 px and the 52 px rail.** Unchanged in value; `diagramLane('rail')` is
  pinned at 1 016 in `column-geometry.spec.ts`.
- **§1.6 — `wide()` chooses the drawing; code that must tell the pin from the rail reads `shape()`.**
  Confirmed by a caller that did not exist when it was written: #82's `foldRule(shape())`. That also
  qualifies §1.4's *the topology reads the same in either* — same `wideLayout`, but the fold's width
  trigger is per lane (164.7 units in the rail, 156.1 pinned), so an algorithm can fold in one shape

- **§1.2 — «the patch's shared ceiling datum».** Shared as a value of the patch — still true. As a line

- **§1.3's second bullet — «no line ever crosses from one branch to another».** As a rule, still the

- **§1.3's fourth bullet — in the grid the zero operator's route is drawn cut, dashed.** Still true and

- **§1.7 — the two widths are `app.ts` and `column-geometry.ts` arithmetic.** The arithmetic now lives

# 3. What changed, block by block

Each block's commit body carries the decisions it had to take; what is repeated here is only the
ones **outside #54's specification**, marked as such, and what the block found on the way. The
session's standing rule, inherited: anything decided during implementation that neither the
specification nor the handoff covers is raised here, never resolved in silence.

## 3.1 Block 0 · The obturador stops contradicting the lock (#55, `88b012d`)

The header's hint is written from the Nota viva — `liveNotes()` — and not from `canMeasure`: the
condition while unsatisfied, `n HELD` once it is. The button's arming is untouched and the spec pins
it from both sides. Decisions outside #54:

- **The satisfied state says what the app can see, not nothing.** #54 specified `n HELD`; what it did
  not say is why blanking the line was refused — the header's job is to state facts, and this is the
  one fact it has about the keyboard. `1 HELD` is not a special case: the figure is the count, not a
  plural.
- **`white-space: nowrap` is what makes «the same height» true.** The `<br>` puts the second line
  there; only `nowrap` stops a longer string putting a third. Asserted as the rule, since jsdom lays
  nothing out.

## 3.2 Block 1 · No stamp without a figure (#56, `790c1bf`)

The node's Hz stamp goes through `PROVENANCE_LABEL` like every other stamp —
`labels[node.hz.provenance]` where it had been a fixed key — and the harmonics legend is keyed to
`AudioService.drawing`. The sweep #54 asked for found no third site. Decisions outside #54:

- **Indexed, not conditional.** No `@if` and no second rule about when a stamp is drawn: there is one
  rule, it lives in the map, and the empty string does the rest.
- **The legend reads `drawing` and not an approximation of it.** In `liveFrame` the curve and the bars
  are the same `null`, both written by `fundamentalHz === null`; `drawing` is forced out of the 4 Hz
  readout the moment it flips, so the legend goes with the last bar rather than a quarter of a second
  later.
- **The axis stays; only the legend goes.** A floor line is what the bars stand on, not a reading —
  the distinction `scope.ts` already makes about its zero line.

## 3.3 Block 2 · A feedback arc only when feedback flows (#57, `76aef88`)

At `FB 0` the arc is `--inert` on `--dash-inactive`, the stub's own pair, in both compositions; the
label keeps its figure. Decisions outside #54:

- **Only a *read* zero cuts it.** `inert` is `value.value === 0` and not `!value.value`: a feedback
  nobody has polled has no amount, which is not the same statement as an amount of none — the
  distinction `fill` already makes in the node. The unread arc stays solid beside its `FB —`.
- **Decided where the amount is, not where the path is.** One line in `OperatorDiagram.feedback` gives
  both layouts the rule; neither layout module learns about amounts.
- **The modifier's position in the sheet is the mechanism**, and the spec pins the order. The selector
  cost 14 bytes over the 6 kB component-style budget and the block it went into paid them back:
  `.routes > path` for `fill: none`, the direct child being what keeps the arrowhead out.

## 3.4 Block 3a · The ranuras decide the composición (#58, `a709ddc`)

The pair the pianist chose is what puts panels on screen; the capture-driven switch and its
announce-delay rule retire; **nothing on this screen moves any more that the pianist did not move.**
The state is all in `Composition`, which no longer injects `AudioService`, `Anchor` or the gateway.
The factory pair is `SCOPE` and empty, so the app opens on the narrow grid — the inversion ADR-0007
exists to record (section 1.1). Decisions outside #54:

- **The waterfall is in one place at a time, and never in none.** The consequence #54 did not see;
  section 5.2.
- **The strip stops being a tab panel and is renamed for it**, and the column with it: `tab-panel/` →
  `bottom-strip/`, `signal-views/` → `glass-column/`. Both had stopped being what they were called,
  which is the `trama` argument (#29) applied to a directory.
- **The captions are a pure module.** `captions.ts` holds the three literals and the two functions,
  so «the same panel says the same thing in a ranura and in the strip» is true by construction and
  not by two templates agreeing.
- **The stored pair is two delimited fields, not JSON** — `SCOPE,`, `SPECTRUM,HARMONICS`, `,` —
  readable in a devtools inspector; anything unparseable falls back to the factory pair through one
  line and costs the pin nothing, which the spec checks.
- **The swap is what makes «no panel in both» structural.** `choose` puts the displaced panel in the
  other ranura rather than refusing, so there is no invariant left to check and no list the chooser
  would have to vary.

## 3.5 Block 3b · The chooser, the rail and the square scope (#62, `594006e`)

The panel's own title is its control — `SPECTRUM ▾`, five entries, the same five in both ranuras;
emptying both leaves a rail rather than a screen with no way back; the scope's move into the column
is stated as a figure — **one cycle was drawn 4.07× wider than the whole trace was tall and is now
0.42×** — and section 4 says what that figure is. Decisions outside #54:

- **The box maths is a module, because the claim is about a box.** `column-geometry.ts` models the
  three lanes, the two halves, the glass and the strip's band as arithmetic; `cycleAspect()` is what
  makes «reads as a wave» checkable, with the divisor the peak-to-peak `scope.ts` actually allows.
- **The rail is for the emptied column and not for the pin.** `KEEP IT BIG` is itself the one press
  back and it is in the header of the panel it is about; a rail beside it would be a second control
  for one state. That is why the shape is three-valued — `ranuras` / `rail` / `gone` — and not
  `wide()`.
- **A handle says what it will put back, and is not persisted.** `Composition.handles` is the memory
  of a gesture — «I emptied this, put it back» — and a relaunch is not that gesture; what survives a
  relaunch is the pair. `restore` goes through `choose`, one rule, one place.
- **An empty ranura keeps its head.** `EMPTY ▾`, because the title is the only way back into that
  half; the frame stays drawn with nothing painting in it, which is what «empty glass» is. The 44 px
  of hit target come out of the chooser's own margin, the pin's trick (#19).
- **Nothing in the list is ever greyed.** Choosing the panel the other half holds swaps them, so
  there is no entry the chooser has to refuse and none it would have to explain.

**The caption sweep #54 ordered — every caption naming a panel's position, `KEEP IT BIG`'s own copy
included — yielded one leftover cleared and two checked and left.** The audio-gone card's «The views
above are stopped, not live» was wrong in position already and, with the ranuras, the views can be
absent entirely; it now says any signal panel on screen is a still, which survives all three shapes.
`KEEP IT BIG` names a size and not a position, and ADR-0007 names the button by that string;
`figures-column`'s «every cell below» is still true. That is the sweep working, not the build
surprising it.

**One gap #62 recorded and did not resolve:** the chooser has no keyboard navigation of its own — five
`<button>`s in a `<ul>`, tabbable, `Escape` closes, no arrow-key roving, no focus return. A menu of
five in a build with no other menu; the commit asks for a decision rather than a default if a second
one ever appears. Unrelated to the retired rule; listed here so it is not lost.
