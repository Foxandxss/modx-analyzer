# 1. One algorithm, two figures (#63, ADR-0007)

`ALG 15` is drawn twice by this app, and the two drawings are both correct. ADR-0007 is the decision
to **keep both and write down what travels between them**, rather than to rewrite either. This
section is the part of it a reader needs in prose: what you carry from one figure to the other, and
what you must not go looking for in the wrong one.

The ADR itself is `docs/adr/0007-dos-dibujos-de-un-algoritmo.md`, accepted 2026-09-10, and it is the
authority — this is its point 3 and point 4 in English, plus what checking it against the shipped
build changed.

## 1.1 Which figure you are looking at, and why that inverted

The **wide** figure (`wide-layout.ts`) teaches topology **by position**: carriers are the nodes that
touch the output bus along the bottom, every arrow points down because a route always drops the
chain depth, each branch gets a contiguous band of columns to itself so no line ever crosses from
one branch to another, and an operator at Level 0 is parked out to the right on a dashed stub that
ends nowhere. None of that is captioned. It is seen.

The **narrow** figure (`layout.ts`) is the depth-sorted 3 × 3 grid: the eight operators sorted
deepest-first and filled three to a row. It is the verified drawing — it has a specification and
`layout.spec.ts` — and it does not claim to teach role by position.

What changed this session is **which one the app opens with**. Until 3a (#58), the wide figure was
the resting state: the app started with no Medida, and with no Medida the algorithm took the whole
width; the narrow grid arrived as a consequence of a capture. Since 3a the two Ranuras decide the
composición, and the factory pair is `SCOPE` and empty — so the glass column is open at the first
frame, and **the narrow grid is now the drawing the app opens with**. The figure that teaches the
most about topology is now the one you have to ask for. That inversion is the whole reason ADR-0007
exists, and it happened sideways, inside a ticket whose subject was the scope's geometry.

## 1.2 What you carry from one figure to the other

Two things, and they cover more than the first draft of the ADR credited them with.

**The node, entire.** There is one component and one template behind both layouts, so the node's
five facts are literally the same in each: the identity (`OP1`…`OP8`) with the role word beside it,
the Level as the height of the luminous fill measured against the patch's shared ceiling datum, the
ratio, the spectral form as a glyph, and the operator's Hz for the live note. With them travel the
one provenance stamp that qualifies everything polled underneath it, the dash that an invalidated
figure leaves inside its kept outline (#56), and the inert open corner. The only difference is how
those facts are arranged inside the box: in the wide figure, when the algorithm's depth leaves less
height than stacking them needs, the node lays them in a row instead (`squat`). Same facts, same
words, different fold.

**The lines.** Every route, every carrier's drop onto the output bus, the bus itself with its
`OUT L/R`, and the feedback arc with its `FB n` are drawn in **both** figures — including the
dashed, inert arc at `FB 0` that #57 added, and the dashed route out of an operator that has been
cut to zero. So **«who modulates whom» is not the wide figure's privilege**: it is in both. The
first draft of the ADR said otherwise, which described the narrow grid as an inventory with no lines
in it. That is not what it is, and the ADR has been corrected.

## 1.3 What you do not carry

The **position**, in four specific ways. Everything on this list exists only in the wide figure, and
a reader who looks for it in the grid will find something that looks like an answer and is not one.

- **Role by place.** In the narrow grid the bottom row is not the carriers' row. The grid fills three
  at a time in depth order, so a row can mix two depths and a carrier can land halfway up. Role is
  said there by the word (`CARR` / `MOD` / `ZERO`) and by the outline's shape, never by where the
  node sits.
- **The shape of a branch.** In the wide figure each connected structure owns a band of columns and
  no line crosses between bands, because there is no line between them to cross. In the grid the
  branches interleave, and nothing marks where one ends and the next begins — a route may even have
  to hop over a neighbour to reach its target.
- **Depth as height.** In the wide figure one row is one depth. In the grid, depth only orders the
  reading — deepest first, by operator number within a depth — and cannot be read back off the
  position.
- **What being at zero looks like.** In the wide figure an operator at Level 0 leaves the branches
  entirely, goes right onto a stub that ends nowhere, and **its routes are not drawn at all**: a line
  carrying nothing is not drawn across the picture. In the grid it keeps its cell and what is drawn
  is its route, **cut** — dashed. Both say «this one is not sounding». They do not say it with the
  same thing, and only one of them says it with position.

## 1.4 How you ask for the wide figure, and the two widths it comes in

By pressing `KEEP IT BIG` in the algorithm panel's own header, or by emptying both Ranuras. Since 3b
(#62) those are **not the same box**, and it is worth knowing which one is on screen:

- **Pinned.** The middle lane closes completely and the diagram takes 1 070 px at a 1 280 viewport.
  There is no rail: the pin is its own handle and it is in the header of the panel it is about.
- **Both ranuras emptied.** The lane collapses to the rail — 52 px, the bench drawer's closed
  vocabulary stood on its end — so the diagram is 1 016 px, and beside it are two handles each
  labelled with the panel one press would put back.

Both are `wide()` and both run the same `wideLayout`; the drawing stretches to whatever room it has
(`preserveAspectRatio="none"`), so the topology reads the same in either. The difference that
matters to a reader is that the second one leaves a way back on screen and the first one does not
need to.

## 1.5 What reconciling them properly would cost, and why it was not done

Taking the positional vocabulary down to the narrow grid is the real reconciliation, and it remains
the right long-term exit. It was not done here because it is a redesign and not a polish pass, and
because of what it would be traded against: the grid is the only one of the two figures that is
verified against the table. Doing it means solving, in a 700 px box, the two problems the wide figure
solves with room — a band per branch and a row per depth — at a scale that has neither the width nor
the height for either. That is its own session with its own measurements in front of it.

Writing the decision down costs a document. Getting the redesign wrong costs the only checked drawing
there is.

## 1.6 What checking the ADR against the build changed

The ADR was written during the grilling that produced #54 — that is, **before** 3a and 3b shipped —
and #63 asks explicitly that whoever closes it check that it still says what was actually built.
Four things did not survive the check, and the ADR now carries the corrected versions:

- **«Who modulates whom» was listed as not carried.** It is carried: the grid draws every route, the
  bus drops, the bus line and the feedback arc. Corrected in points 3 and 4, and in the paragraph
  that describes the narrow figure.
- **The list of the node's facts named the stamp and omitted the operator's Hz.** The five facts are
  identity-and-role, Level, ratio, spectral form and Hz; the stamp and the ceiling datum travel with
  them but are not among the five. Corrected in point 3.
- **«The wide figure is reached by `KEEP IT BIG` or by emptying both ranuras» was written before the
  rail existed.** It is still true, but the two routes now produce different widths — 1 070 px and
  1 016 px — and one of them leaves a rail on screen. Corrected in point 5.
- **The discarded alternative «factory pair with both ranuras empty» argued that nobody would see a
  signal panel «until they discover the handle».** With #62's rail there is nothing to discover: the
  handle is on screen and it is labelled with what it will restore. The alternative stays discarded,
  but for the honest reason — the app would open with no signal drawn and one press in front of the
  first one — and not for a cost the build no longer has.

One thing was checked and left alone: `composition.wide()` is still what chooses between the two
drawings (`operator-diagram.ts`), exactly as the ADR says. What changed underneath it is that it is
now derived from the column's three-valued `shape()` — `ranuras` / `rail` / `gone` — so code that
needs to tell the pin from the rail must read `shape()`. `wide()` deliberately gives them the same
answer, because for the drawing they are the same answer.

## 1.7 What this section does not claim

**Nothing here was measured on the keyboard, and none of it needed to be.** Every claim is about
which figure draws what, and each is readable in `layout.ts`, `wide-layout.ts` and
`operator-diagram.html`. The two widths — 1 070 px and 1 016 px — are the grid's own arithmetic at a
1 280 viewport (`app.ts`, and `column-geometry.ts` for the 52 px rail), not a measurement of a
screenshot.

What is still owed is the thing this section cannot settle: whether a pianist who learns the
topology in the wide figure actually recognises it in the grid. That is the question the
reconciliation would answer, and it needs someone in front of the real screen with a real
Performance — not a document.
