# 2. Where the handoff or the prompt was wrong and the build was right

Eight of these were predicted by #25 and are confirmed; three more were found while implementing.
Each says what the paper claimed, what the build says, and what makes the build's version checkable.

## 2.1 The scope diagnosis: autocorrelation, not zero crossings

**The paper** (`prompt.md` §2.1): «the trigger has locked onto a sub-harmonic, most likely by
counting zero crossings, which bright FM crosses many times per period».

**The build**: there has never been a zero-crossing count in the frequency path. `estimatePeriod` is
normalised autocorrelation and always was; what it does on a bright FM timbre is pick a **sub-multiple
of the period**, which is why `43.8 Hz` is 349.23/8 rather than a crossing artefact. Same symptom,
different cause — and a different fix: a crossing counter would have been replaced, and instead
autocorrelation **changed jobs**. It no longer names the frequency; it verifies the note the keyboard
says is held, over the existing 0.6 threshold (`PERIODIC_ENOUGH`, `packages/modx-dsp/src/scope.ts:63`).

**Checkable**: the three harmonic golden vectors correlate 1.000 at C4 and lock — `fmx-ratio2-modhigh`
included, whose spectral peak is the **9th harmonic**, the case that breaks anything trusting the
loudest line. `fmx-ratio1414` correlates **−0.199** at the note and is refused with
`NO LOCK · pitch unstable`, which is the true answer for a waveform that genuinely does not repeat
there. Four vectors, `scope.spec.ts` («los vectores de oro»), commit `ab0d5dd`.

## 2.2 The floor: one formula, two signals

Section 3 is this in full. In one line: the paper asked which of −58 and −104 is right, and the
answer is **both, for their signal**, with one formula that was never in question.

## 2.3 `FE Line` is the feedback row

Section 4. `GLOSSARY.md` §4b asked for a rename of a readout nobody could define; the readout is
`FB n · OP n` and the words are Yamaha's.

## 2.4 384 requests, 383 answers

**The paper**: `--reread-total: 416` in both token files, `383` in `prompt.md`.

**The build**: 416 was the phase 0c sweep, which asked every `al` of `ah` 48 and 49 one byte at a
time. The app reads **one address per parameter, 384 of them**, and one of those answers nothing.
So the token is the number of **requests the build issues** — 384 — and 383 is the answered count
the strip prints (`REREAD · 383 OF 384`). Hiding the silent address inside a 383 token would have
made the readout unable to ever show a complete pass.

**Checkable**: measured on the MODX8 on 2026-09-08 under #13 — 1.7 s and 383 of 384, four times
running, and the same 1.7 s with a chord held right through the change. That figure is session 1's;
what session 2 changed is the token that disagreed with it (`b7777ef`).

## 2.5 The header's audio line was already there, and was two facts of three

**The paper**: implied a new pipe for the `LIVE` pill's second line.

**The build**: `Line (MODX) · 44100 Hz` was already on screen, read from the opened stream — the
prompt's own audit said so and was right. What was missing was the third fact: `DeviceInfo` has
carried `channels` since the bridge was written and the Rust side never passed it on, because
`Connection::set_audio` took `(String, u32)`. It takes the whole `DeviceInfo` now (#35, `39cd452`),
so the line is `Line (MODX) · 44100 Hz · 2 ch` and every one of the three facts comes off the stream
the app actually opened.

## 2.6 `CONCERNS.md` §23.1's premise is false

**The paper**: «the anchor cannot poll inside a capture», therefore void the anchor dot while the
shutter is open.

**The build**: the capture takes the **already recorded** last 1.5 s out of the ring and the anchor
never stops. The blind spot as drawn does not exist, so the fix as drawn would have voided a dot for
no reason. The **real** hole is the other end of the window: it reaches 1.486 s into the past while
the anchor takes up to a second to see a change, so a Performance changed in the second before the
press yielded a capture of the old sound, drawn and attributed to the new patch. That is the one
failure the whole application exists to prevent, and it is closed by `ui/src/app/provenance/aval.ts`
— a pure function over the beat history, not a change to the anchor's cadence (#38, `dea0d75`).

## 2.7 «fc from the last capture» was written into a lock priority no fit can reach

**The paper**: the scope's lock priority starts with fc from the last capture.

**The build**: no Bessel code, no fitted index and no Level→index mapping exist. `setCapturedFc` is
written, tested and **called by nothing** (`ab0d5dd`), and the fourth refusal that would go with it —
`no capture yet` — is deliberately not shipped, because dead copy is a string the next sweep has to
explain. The branch lands with #51.

## 2.8 The waterfall's 460 and 462 ms

**The paper**: `14 tramas · 0 → 460 ms` in the caption, `462 ms` in five doc comments,
`22 FRAMES · 0 → 712 ms` in `DESIGN.md`.

**The build**: rows are pushed only when there is a curve, so silences are skipped and count × hop is
not the span of anything. 14 × 30 ms is **420 ms** — neither 460 nor 462 — and the true span is the
difference between the first and last row's stamps, which after a rest is much larger. The caption is
computed now (`WATERFALL · n FRAMES · 0 → m ms`); the depth stays 14 and `DESIGN.md`'s 22 is marked
an illustration (#32 `56cc0b3`, #49 `46a041c`).

## 2.9 `CONCERNS.md` §31's arithmetic bound was low in both halves

**The paper**: «up to 6 depth levels with up to 4 parallel branches at one level … no real algorithm
can exceed it», with the hope that the histogram would let the surface be *tightened*.

**The build**: computed over the 88 transcribed topologies (#40, `1ef3aab`), the **66** needs eight

# 3. The floor question

**The question** (`prompt.md` §2.1): the build reports `FLOOR −58 dB` where the phase-0 spikes
measured `−104 dB`; forty-six decibels is a lot, and the floor decides whether a line is real.

**The answer: both figures are right, for their signal, out of one formula that was never in
question.** There is exactly one floor formula in the repo — the **median of all bins**, Hann,
computed on the live window at 4 096 and on the capture at 65 536 — and its value moves with **how
much of the spectrum is content**, because the median bin is a property of the whole spectrum and
not of the noise in it.

Session 1's own table shows the trend across four signals, all relative to the peak, all at 4 096
(`docs/results/2026-09-07-fase1-sesion1.md`, «Lo que sigue midiéndose sólo sobre los vectores»):

| golden vector | floor, median bin rel. peak | what the timbre is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `fmx-1op-sine` | **−104.8 dB** | one operator, a pure tone: 32 578 of 32 769 bins are floor |
| `fmx-ratio2-modlow` | −99.9 dB | four odd partials |
| `fmx-ratio2-modhigh` | −89.0 dB | odd partials to the 21st; the peak is the 9th |
| `fmx-ratio1414` | −82.8 dB | eleven inharmonic lines |

So the brighter the sound, the higher the floor reads, over 22 dB of spread on four synthetic files
with **no noise in them at all**. The live USB stream then adds two things the offline WAVs do not
have: the keyboard's own converters, and a spectrum with far more of its bins occupied. Session 1
measured the live spectrum's floor at **−68 to −88 dB depending on the timbre** (`MIRAR` on the
MODX8, 2026-09-07/08); the prompt's −58 dB is the same reading on a brighter patch played harder.
**The −104 was never the live floor**: it is the extreme of the four vectors, the one signal with
almost nothing in it.

**What changed as a result (#30, `3b7ff05`).** `FLOOR` is now **absolute dBFS** everywhere, because
a figure relative to the peak rose and fell with how hard somebody played and could not be compared
with the peak beside it, with the floor on another panel, or with anything. The formula did not
change; its reference did.

- The DSP proves the property rather than a number: `spectrum.spec.ts` builds a fixed noise floor
  under a note played **12 dB louder** and shows the absolute reading holding to a tenth of a
  decibel while the old relative one moved the whole 12.
- The same vector reads **−104.8 dB rel. peak and −130.4 dBFS**, and both figures are in the code
  beside each other so nobody compares the two references and concludes the build is broken. Every
  −104 / −105 quote in the repo now carries «rel. al pico».
- **The chips did not move and that is the proof the arithmetic is unchanged.** The partial picker's
  cutoff was `peakDb + floorDb + OVER_FLOOR_DB` on a relative floor, which is `median + 8`; it is
  `floorDb + OVER_FLOOR_DB` now, which is the same `median + 8`. `NOT A HARMONIC` fires exactly
  where it fired, which is why the generator comb is still marked on a live floor sixty decibels
  above the vectors': that threshold was always the signal's own.
- `NO_FRAME.floorDb` is `−Infinity`, not 0 (§6.4): as a relative figure 0 meant «the floor is the
  peak»; in dBFS it would mean full scale, which is a claim about a frame in which nothing entered.
- `compareWindows` keeps its figure **relative** and says so in the field names
  (`relFloorDbA/B`, row `suelo A / B (dB rel. al pico)`): that procedure exists to be read beside
  phase 0's −100 to −109 dB.

**Still owed**: the live absolute figure. Nothing in this session read a floor off the keyboard, so
what the pill says on a real MODX8 in dBFS is unmeasured. Section 7.1.

# 5. Measurements taken this session

ADR-0006's rule applies to every figure below: **method, and how many samples it speaks for.** Two
of the four figures #53 asked for could not be taken, and they are here with the reason rather than
absent.

## 5.1 The 88-algorithm maxima — computed, not measured (#40, `1ef3aab`)

**Method.** Computed over the 88 topologies transcribed in `crates/modx-midi/src/algorithms.rs` from
the MODX Data List's Algorithm Chart (pages 182–185). All 88 entries are `documentado`: this is
arithmetic on the paper table, **not** a keyboard reading. Depth is `Topology::chain_depth` (a
carrier is 0; an operator is one deeper than the deepest thing it modulates). A branch is the
undirected reading of the routes, `Topology::branches`, so the feedback loop — drawn as a rectangle
around boxes already joined — is an edge in neither. **It speaks for all 88.**

`measures_the_worst_case_the_wide_composition_has_to_hold` asserts each bound per algorithm **and**
asserts it is reached, so a constant no algorithm touches fails as loudly as one an algorithm
exceeds.

| maximum | value | reached by |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| depth (`MAX_DEPTH`) | **7** — eight rows | the **66** alone, the single chain of eight |
| operators on one row (`MAX_ROW`) | **8** | the **1** alone, eight carriers on the bus |
| operators of one branch on one row (`MAX_BRANCH_ROW`) | **7** | the **68** alone, seven modulators into Op8 |
| branches side by side on one row (`MAX_PARALLEL_BRANCHES`) | **8** | the **1** alone |

Histograms over the 88 — depth: 0×1, 1×26, 2×37, 3×17, 4×5, 5×1 (the 37), 7×1 (the 66). Branches:
1×6, 2×11, 3×26, 4×29, 5×11, 6×3, 7×1, 8×1.

**And the definition the wide composition rests on holds in all 88**: every operator at chain depth 0
is exactly a member of the `carriers` list, entry by entry
(`every_operator_that_modulates_nobody_is_a_portadora`, #42). Without it the bottom row could one
day hold an operator touching nothing, and «a carrier is a node that touches the output bus» would
be a sentence with an exception.

**What it decided**: `CONCERNS.md` §31's bound is low in both halves (§2.9), and the node in the wide
composition cannot be a constant size (§6.15).

## 5.2 The waterfall span — synthetic only (#32, `56cc0b3`)

**Method.** The audio service driven by the fake audio worker at the existing seam: fourteen frames
with a curve, a rest of twenty-seven, then one more frame. **One scripted run, deterministic**; it
speaks for the arithmetic and for nothing about a real phrase.

**Figure: 1 110 ms over fourteen rows**, where count × hop would have claimed 420. That is the whole
point of the stamps: the span is `last − first` over `LiveView.stamps`, one stamp per drawn row,
taken from the bloque's own `sentAtMicros` (the audio's clock) and not from a `performance.now()`
read when the message happened to be handled.

**Not measured**: the span on a phrase played on the MODX8, which is what #53 §5 asks for. No
keyboard. Section 7.1.

## 5.3 The capture Aval hold — not measured, but bounded (#37 `a915542`, #38 `dea0d75`)

#38's fourth acceptance criterion is p50 / p99 of the shutter's hold on the real keyboard. **It was
not taken: no keyboard.** What exists instead:

- **The synthetic path, end to end at the audio-service seam**, with the fake gateway's beats
  scripted in the order the native side would emit them: a window bracketed by two `same` beats is
  drawn; a window whose following beat says `changed` is discarded unseen; a window with no
  post-window beat yet stays pending and draws nothing.
- **The design bound.** The press files the same out-of-turn request the wide ring makes
  (`requestAnchorBeat`, asserted), so the hold is **one beat at most**. `aval_gap`
  (`src-tauri/src/anchor.rs:310`, two cargo tests) puts that at **250 ms at an idle 40 ms pass and
  a whole second at a 200 ms pass under notes** — ADR-0005's gap rule, extracted and unchanged.

So the hold is known to be ≤ 1 s by construction and unmeasured in fact. Section 7.1.

## 5.4 The ring cadence after the token change — not measured (#26, `b7777ef`)

#53 §5 asks for a confirmation that 10 Hz still holds. **Not taken: no keyboard.** What can be said
without one:

- The 10.0–10.4 Hz the token now carries is the **owner's** reading of the running build against the
  MODX8, quoted in `prompt.md:262`, and it replaces a documented 12.2 Hz that session 1 had already
  half-confirmed at 11.5–12 Hz over 42 addresses (`LOS OCHO · N Hz`, #11, 2026-09-07).
- **Nothing this session changed the ring's work.** The wide ring polls the same 40 addresses
  (5 parameters × 8 operators) plus the header; no address was added, and #50 exists precisely so
  that `CLEAN CHAIN`'s eight are not added without deciding the budget first. What #37 added per
  pass is one `modx://anchor-beat` event and a one-shot flag read inside the existing wait.
- What moved is the **token and its one code twin**: `STALE_WIDE_FLOOR_MS` 330 → 400 ms. It is the
  only constant in the repo that mirrored one of the five values, and the crossing point is now
  written into `freshness.spec.ts`: at 12.2 Hz an idle pass of 84 ms gave 336 ms and the ×4
  multiplier governed; at 10 Hz the same pass sits under the 400 ms floor and **the floor governs
  in silence**. That is a behaviour change at rest and it is why the figure needs re-taking.

## 5.5 The scope's lock, on the golden vectors (#31, `ab0d5dd`)

## 6.2 English on the designed surfaces (#27, `42ab98e`)

- **The provenance stamps lost two words, not two states.** `GLOSSARY.md` §2 says stale is not a
  fourth source — it is `POLLED` plus age, already drawn twice by the broken outline and the seconds
  — and an invalidated figure is a dash inside its kept outline, so a word beside a blank would
  label the blank. `PROVENANCE_LABEL` keeps both keys and renders `POLLED` for stale and nothing for
  invalidated; every `data-stamp` assertion is untouched.
- **The `Mode` type took its §6 words immediately**, ahead of #34: block 1 deferred the `A/B` and
  `APRENDER` buttons, but #34 keeps all three type values and only stops rendering two, so leaving
  the value Spanish would have left one Spanish word rendering for no gain.

## 6.3 English on the bench (#28, `fa277ef`)

- **`PortError`'s four messages were swept with the dump reasons.** `could not request the dump:
  {error}` interpolates a `PortError`, so leaving those Spanish would have put half a Spanish
  sentence in the alert strip on the one path the criterion is about. No other error type was swept.
- **The exported window keeps `-sondeo-` / `-sin-sondeo-` in its file name** — §1.1, and the one
  Spanish word that still renders. It is an artefact name, tied to #14's documented procedure and to
  `MODX_SONDEO` / `MODX_SIN_SONDEO` in the comparison spec, and renaming it would orphan the windows
  already on disk and the script that reads them.

## 6.4 `FLOOR` in dBFS (#30, `3b7ff05`)

- **`NO_FRAME.floorDb` is `−Infinity`, not 0**, and **`compareWindows` keeps its figure relative and
  renames its fields to say so**. Both are in section 3, because both are consequences of moving one
  reference rather than choices about copy.
- **The level-independence test lives in the DSP package, not at the panel.** A synthetic pure tone
  has no floor — its median bin is leakage, which scales with the tone — so a panel spec would have
  asserted the opposite of the rule. The DSP spec builds a fixed noise floor under a note played
  12 dB louder instead.

## 6.5 The scope (#31, `ab0d5dd`)

- **The two frequencies were one field and are now two.** `BridgeFrame.frequencyHz` fed both the
  caption and the spectrum's axis; `scope` is what the caption is written from and `drawnHz` is the
  note the axis is drawn against, still falling back to `estimatePeriod` when the MIDI port is gone,
  because an axis in multiples of a note needs *some* note. The fallback reads the last three bloques
  it always read (`AXIS_BLOCKS`) and not the scope's longer buffer, because `estimatePeriod` measures
  from the start of what it is given.
- **The history grew from 3 bloques to 7 and the trace is cut from its newest end.** Four cycles plus
  the period the first crossing may cost is five periods of A0, 181.8 ms; seven bloques are 210. The
  old code drew the **oldest** cycles of its buffer, which is how a still shape can be a picture of
  the past.
- **Under the floor no samples cross the seam at all**, and digital silence is answered the same way
  and checked **before** «no held note», because what is on screen is the floor.
- **`no capture yet` is not shipped** (§2.7), and `SCOPE_CYCLES` is the only copy of the four: the
  DSP cuts to it and the template reads it.
- **The sub-harmonic hole is left open, by design.** A keyboard holding C3 while a C4 sounds locks at
  C3 and draws eight cycles calling them four. Verification cannot see it — a C4 correlates perfectly
  at the C3 period — and the keyboard is the authority on which note is down.

## 6.6 The waterfall (#32, `56cc0b3`)

- **With no rows the span clause is absent, not zero**: `WATERFALL · 0 FRAMES`. A picture of nothing
  has no first row and no last, so `0 → 0 ms` would be a figure standing where there is no
  measurement. One row *does* span nothing and says `0 → 0 ms`, which is the true reading of a single
  instant. The zero-frames form has no §6 row and no `DESIGN.md` quote.
- **The caption moves at the readout's 4 Hz**, with the panel's two existing exceptions (it also
  refreshes the moment the live view starts or stops drawing, which is exactly the boundary a silence
  puts in the picture). A frame count re-rendered 33 times a second would be the counting-up the
  design forbids.

## 6.7 The measured column (#33, `2ef808f`)

- **`WORST PARTIAL`'s unit line reads `dB off prediction`**, not the bare `dB` #25 names. The string
  is drawn piece 8d's own and it does the job `DESIGN.md` §10.1 assigns unit lines — «the unit lines
  are doing real work» — by saying both what the figure is measured in and what the gap is between.
  No copy was invented.
- **The resting column carries no note at all.** `onPatchChanged` used to set
  `NOT MEASURED IN THIS SOUND`; it sets `null` now, because #25 says that case is covered by the
  resting state plus the header. The string exists in exactly one place on screen: the anchor's third
  state. Only the two notes #25 names survive, plus `capturing` while the shutter is open.

## 6.8 An unbuilt mode is not a disabled button (#34, `7bbebed`)

- **The segmented control's frame went with its two dead thirds.** A border and a filled «selected»
  state around one static label still read as pressable — the same lie at a third the size. What is
  left is typography in the vocabulary of the readouts beside it.
- **`role="group"` and its `Mode` accessibility label were dropped, not translated.** With no controls
  inside, a group of one static string is furniture being announced.
- **8 px stands against the token file's own comment**, which is §2.11.

## 6.9 The header (#35, `39cd452`)

- **No chain chip is drawn.** The slot in the canonical order is reserved and nothing occupies it: a
  chip drawn void would be a readout that no press, no note and no patch change could ever fill,
  which is #34's rule applied to a readout. Drawn piece 8a's own `AFTER` header has none either. It
  goes between `BUILD` and the `LIVE` pill when #50 lands.
- **`MAIN L/R` is still not shown.** The stream can report its name, its rate and its channel count;
  it cannot report where the keyboard is routing, and inventing the fourth fact in the one line whose
  point is that nothing in it is invented would be this session's own failure. It stays `DOCUMENTED`
  routing for the check screen (#52).

## 6.10 Harmonics (#36, `0e0cc05`)

- **The column's `NOT A HARMONIC` stays a plain tag and is not replaced by the chip.** Every chip
  carries its frequency and a chip without one is now unrepresentable (`hz` is a required input); the
  column's partial table is not a chip, because its row's **first column is the frequency** and the
  tag would sit twelve pixels right of the number it repeats. The words are shared — `NOT_A_HARMONIC`
  is exported beside the component — so the string exists once.
- **The `PREDICTED` legend entry is held back rather than drawn void**: a key naming a curve nothing
  can draw sends the eye hunting for it. It joins with the overlay (#51).
- **The rule was narrowed, not lifted**: «no Bessel overlay» read as a permanent ban; the real rule is
  *no predicted overlay until there is a capture with a fitted index*, which a fit can satisfy.

## 6.11 The anchor's beats across the seam (#37, `a915542`)

- **Two instants, not one.** A pass costs ~40 ms idle and ~200 ms under notes, and what it vouches
  for is everything before it *started*; the end is only when the answer became known. Collapsing
  them would hand the aval a fifth of a second of slack in the one condition — somebody playing —
  where a Performance is most likely to be changed underneath.
- **A history and not the last beat.** `anchorBeats` keeps the last sixteen, oldest first, ~4 s at the
  250 ms floor: #38's rule needs *both* ends of the window, and a signal holding only the newest would
  have the older of the two already overwritten by the time the shutter asks.
- **An incomplete pass carries no name.** It read no whole name, and reporting the last known one
  would be the app claiming a reading it did not take.
- **`beat_asked` is a second flag rather than a share of `aval_wanted`**, because the ring rewrites
  that one once per turn and a request from across the IPC would be erased within milliseconds. It is
  one-shot and cleared *before* the pass, so a request arriving while twenty addresses are in flight
  survives into the next one; a standing flag would hold the anchor at its 250 ms floor for the rest
  of the session.
- **The fake deliberately does not beat when a beat is requested.** The times are the whole point, and
  a driver that invented them would let a test pass on times no keyboard produces.

## 6.12 The capture's Aval (#38, `dea0d75`)

- **The shutter is dead while the polling switch is thrown, and a capture already in flight is
  discarded rather than held.** #14's instrument exists so the app *stops noticing* a Performance
  change; with it on, no capture could ever be vouched for. Arming the button there would open a
  shutter whose table nothing could draw, and holding the in-flight capture would leave the shutter
  busy for the rest of the session waiting on something switched off. `canMeasure` reads
  `polling().paused` alongside the audio, and the button is disabled exactly as it is before the
  first bloque. #14's own comparison is unaffected: it runs through `exportWindow` + `compareWindows`.
- **A capture with no beat closed before its window is discarded, not drawn.** #25 names the
  `Changed` case; it does not say what happens at launch, when the ring can fill before the anchor
  has looked twice. The app was not watching when those samples entered, so it has nothing to say
  about what was sounding — a refusal, not a pass. The same reasoning covers a post-window pass
  answering `first`.

## 6.13 The operator node (#39, `f0af8f1`)

- **The glyph's stroke width is a constant, because `Spectral Skirt` is not on the wide ring.**
  `DESIGN.md` §9 gives the 18 × 14 drawing a fifth channel — Skirt as stroke width, eight widths for
  the parameter's eight positions — and `49 op 0A` is not one of the five addresses the ring reads per
  operator. A width drawn off a value nobody polled is the failure this session exists to remove, so
  every glyph is drawn at `--rule-min` until the Skirt is actually read. The channel is documented in
  `spectral-glyph.ts` rather than left as a silent omission.
- **A form nobody read draws the dash, not a Sine.** `Sine` is the form every operator of
  `Init Normal (FM-X)` starts in, which is exactly why defaulting to it would be a claim rather than a
  blank. `spectralGlyph` answers `null` for an unread form and for one outside the seven.
- **The node's `POLLED` stamp moves to its head; `PREDICTED` does not move.** The stamp qualifies
  everything under it — role, Level, ratio, form — and the ratio's line is where the glyph now goes;
  #19's rule survives whole, never two stamps on one baseline. `PREDICTED` stays on the Hz line
  because it qualifies the one figure in the node that is arithmetic. Drawn piece 8c shows the node
  with no stamp words at all; that is not what shipped, because #39's own list of five facts says the
  Hz is stamped.
- **The ceiling datum is named once, in the legend, and the corner is not named at all.** A dashed
  line crossing eight nodes with nothing to say what it is of has to be guessed, so the legend gains
  `THE LOUDEST OPERATOR IN THIS PATCH`. The corner's line — `5 of 43 facts shown · the corner opens
  the other 38` — is held back until the editor exists, which is #34's rule applied to a caption.
- **The operator's Hz drops a decimal past 1 kHz and both past 10 kHz.** A tenth of a hertz on a
  133 kHz prediction is not a figure, it is the two characters that would push `PREDICTED` off the
  node.

## 6.14 The algorithm takes the room (#41, `6755cd3`)

- **The wide composition is 1 070 px, not the 1 232 the ticket names, because the figures column
  stays.** 1232 + 208 exceeds the 1 280 the viewport is fixed at. The column had to win: it is the one
  thing on screen that says what a capture would fill, and `before any capture` is exactly the state
  it was drawn for, so removing it in the composition that state produces would make #33's invitation
  unreachable. The 1 232 × 400 is not lost — it is the drawing's own coordinate space, stretched by
  `preserveAspectRatio="none"`.
- **The regrowth waits for the change flash as well as the reread strip.** #25 names the strip; the
  flash is named in the same sentence of the specification and is the same problem. A Performance
  change already puts two things on screen and a panel resizing under them would be a third, with
  nothing to say which was about which. Both ends terminate on their own, so the wait cannot hang.
  **Shrinking never waits**: the panels come back the instant the capture lands, because that is the
  consequence of the press and should read as one.
- **Drawn piece 8f's chip `NOTHING MEASURED YET — THE ALGORITHM HAS THE ROOM` is not built**: #33
  draws `NOTHING MEASURED YET` at the head of the figures column, which is on screen in this
  composition, and a second copy would be the same sentence twice.
- **The pin is `localStorage` behind a `try`, and it defaults to up.** `CONCERNS.md` §30 wonders
  whether `KEEP IT BIG` should default *down* after the first capture of a session; nothing here
  decides that, and a webview that refuses storage costs the pin and nothing else.

## 6.15 Role reads from position (#42, `cf3c0dc`) — the layout

- **The node stops being a constant size, and below a floor it lays its facts in a row.** Eight rows
  of the narrow 108-unit node is 864 in a 400-unit box (§2.10), so the row pitch is what the depth
  leaves, the node takes it, and under the 90 units #39's five facts need to stack the layout answers
  `squat`: the node lays them in a row with up to 380 units of width. **The layout decides it and not
  a CSS container query** — the layout is the only thing that knows how many rows had to share the
  height, and one rule in units beats a rule in units plus a second one in pixels that can disagree
  about the same node. It is squat from four rows down, which is 24 of the 88, algorithm 2 among them.
- **Under three rows the node stops growing, and the room the depth did not use is air.** Three is the
  narrow composition's own row count, so a shallow algorithm draws a node about the size it has at
  700 px. Without the floor the **1** would draw eight nodes as tall as the panel, and the drawing
  would change size every time the Performance did.
- **A parked operator's routes are not drawn at all**, and it gets a dead end instead: a dashed drop
  closed by a bar. In the narrow grid the routes are drawn cut; here the node is off the branches, so
  a line back into the grid would cross the whole drawing to claim a path that carries nothing. It
  never reaches the bus and never takes the bus row, because touching the bus is the entire definition
  of a carrier here.
- **The stub takes as many columns as it needs to keep the row pitch**, and the total never passes
  eight: at most `8 − k` columns are placed and the stub is `ceil(k / rows)`. Six at zero is the phase
  0c patch — an ordinary two-operator sound — and it draws two nodes on the left and six parked on the
  right.
- **Branches get contiguous bands, ordered by the operator that names them, and never cross**, so the
  same algorithm is always the same picture whatever the ring read first.
- **Drawn piece 8f's four annotations are not app copy.** `ABOVE THE BUS — THESE FEED SOMEBODY` and
  its three siblings are the sheet teaching its own reader; the composition exists so that position
  says it without a caption.

## 6.16 The bench drawer (#48, `e88d312`)

- **The chips are tabs and one instrument is on screen at a time.** #25 says «five chips … grouping
  the code's readouts» and does not say whether the open drawer shows one group or five stacked.
  Drawn piece 8g settles it, and the choice is what lets the open handle carry a line that is only
  true of one panel: `TICKET #44 · THIS PANEL GOES AWAY WHEN THE CAPTURE PATH IS TRUSTED`. Retirement
  stays a deletion and not a refactor: closing a ticket removes one `@switch` case, one name from
  `BenchGroup` and one entry from `BENCH_CHIPS`.
- **Nothing inside the drawer is rendered while it is shut** — not hidden, absent, the rule the two
  live panels already follow in the wide composition. A readout nobody can see is still a computed
  that runs on every pass, and these are fed at 33 Hz.
- **Alert becomes ink, not nothing.** Four facts in these strips exist *because* they change what a
  run means: a negative latency leg, a starved generator, the anchor paused, an offset that moved
  between two sweeps. Dropping the distinction would lose the reading; keeping `--alert` would break
  «nothing in the drawer nags». They are drawn in `--ink-primary` against the `--ink-inert` the strips
  are written in, and the spec asserts the absence of `var(--alert)` from the compiled stylesheet with
  each of the five chips open in turn.
- **The ticket number is drawn at `--text-micro` like the name, quieter in ink.** The sheet sets it a
  pixel smaller; the app's floor for anything that is *read* is 10 px, and a number whose job is to
  say which ticket to close is read.
- **No console chip.** The SysEx console is not built, and an un-ticketed chip that opens nothing is
  #34's lie. It docks into this handle when it exists — and it is the one chip with no ticket, which
  is how the one that stays is told from the five that go.

## 6.17 `trama` becomes `frame` (#29, `a2ca013`)

- **`frame` was already taken by the PCM frame, so three locals gave way instead of the field.**
  `BridgeFrame` holds the analysis under `.frame`, and a variable called `frame` holding one reads
  `frame.frame` at every use, so the variable moved: `bridged`, and `message` for the `FrameMessage`.
  **`BLOCK_FRAMES` and `callbackFrames` did not move**: those are the device's frames, a different
  noun that shares a word.
- **`BlockMeter`'s ring of costs is `frameCosts`, not `frames`**, which would have put a
  `Float64Array` of milliseconds beside `callbackFrames` named after the samples it is not.
- **The three `performance` marks are renamed although they are strings**: their whole job is to be
  read in a profile, and a profile saying `trama` while every symbol says `frame` is the split the
  ticket closes.
- **Four English test titles lose the Spanish noun; the Spanish ones keep it.** Those four were
  English sentences carrying the one word being renamed; the specs still titled in Spanish are prose,
  and no other Spanish identifier is renamed.

## 6.18 The handoff commit (#49, `46a041c`)
