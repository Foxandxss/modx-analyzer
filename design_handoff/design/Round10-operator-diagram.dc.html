Three questions that compete for the same pixels: 10b what form the ceiling reference takes, 10c what a card's width is for, 10d whether eight rows get drawn in full — and 10f, the three conditions the answer carries. They are answered together, and the answer is one move: in the wide drawing Level lies along the axis its composition states — height in the grid, length in both of the wide boxes — the ceiling becomes a vertical rule, and the chain folds its facts instead of its positions. 10d Question 3 — fold the facts, never the positions algorithm 66 at the boot composition, lane 1 016 px
The objection to folding is right as stated: height on the page is depth, so spending rows spends the only axis that carries meaning without text. So do not spend rows — spend facts. The deepest operators keep their row, their order and their place above what they modulate, and give up the ratio, the glyph, the Hz and the stamp. Level and identity stay, against the same vertical ceiling rule, at full resolution. A press unfolds them, and what a press gives you is exactly today's drawing.

WHAT THE FOLD KEEPS
Carrier on the bus at the bottom; every arrow down; one row per depth for the four nearest the output; the folded four still above what they modulate and still in depth order. Their Levels are still measured against the same rule at the same offset, so the loudest operator in the patch is still found by eye — 99 at OP1 is visible inside the band. What you lose is its ratio, its glyph, its Hz and its stamp, and one press gets them back. The band wears one stamp, the weakest of the four — not a new rule, just weakest() applied one level up, because a value whose provenance is behind a press is a claim nothing is backing.
